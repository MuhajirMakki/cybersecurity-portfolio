# Sound the Alarm: Detection and Response

## Background

Incident detection and response is an important part of cybersecurity. Security professionals need to identify incidents, understand how they occurred, document their findings, and support the recovery process.

This portfolio activity is part of the Google Cybersecurity Certificate course. It focuses on documenting a ransomware incident using an Incident Handler’s Journal.

## Activity Overview

In this activity, I reviewed a security incident involving a small healthcare clinic in the United States. Employees were unable to access medical records and other important files because the organization's systems had been affected by ransomware.

The incident started with targeted phishing emails containing malicious attachments. After the attachments were downloaded, malware was installed and the attackers deployed ransomware to encrypt critical files. The attackers then demanded money in exchange for a decryption key.

I used the Incident Handler’s Journal format to document the incident, identify the five W's, and record additional questions that could support further investigation.

## Objective

- Document the ransomware incident using an Incident Handler’s Journal.
- Identify who caused the incident, what happened, when and where it occurred, and why it happened.
- Record relevant details clearly for future investigation and reference.
- Consider possible prevention measures and incident response actions.

## Tool(s) Used

- **Incident Handler’s Journal** — Used to document the incident and organize its key details.
- **Google Docs / Microsoft Word** — Used to prepare and save the journal entry.

*Note: No technical security tools were used directly in this scenario-based activity.*

## Incident Handler’s Journal

**Date:** October 20, 2026  
**Entry:** #1

### Description

Documenting a ransomware incident affecting a healthcare clinic. The incident disrupted business operations and prevented employees from accessing critical patient records and other files.

### The 5 W's

**Who caused the incident?**

An organized group of attackers was responsible for the ransomware incident. The scenario describes them as a group known to target healthcare and transportation organizations.

**What happened?**

The attackers used ransomware to encrypt the clinic's critical files. Employees could not access medical records and other necessary resources, and a ransom note demanded money in exchange for the decryption key.

**When did the incident occur?**

The incident was reported on a Tuesday at approximately 9:00 a.m. The exact calendar date was not provided in the scenario.

**Where did the incident happen?**

The incident affected a small healthcare clinic in the United States that provides primary-care services.

**Why did the incident happen?**

The attackers gained access to the organization's network through targeted phishing emails containing malicious attachments. The ransom demand suggests that financial gain was a motive.

### Additional Notes

1. How could the clinic reduce the risk of similar attacks through employee phishing awareness training, email filtering, and endpoint security?
2. Were reliable backups available to restore the encrypted files, and what steps were needed to contain the attack and recover operations?

## Key Learning

This activity helped me understand the importance of documenting a security incident in a structured way. The five W's help organize the available information, while additional notes identify questions that may need further investigation.

Accurate incident documentation supports investigation, communication, recovery, and future prevention.
